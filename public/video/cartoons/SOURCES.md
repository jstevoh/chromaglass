# The cartoons that ship with ChromaGlass

Nine early cartoons, each the whole film looped, played as dye or as the projector's
film (`src/lib/cartoons.ts`, PLAN 14ab-2). The first four are black and white; the
five after them, added 2026-10-27, are in colour. This file records where each copy
came from and why it is free to ship. Add a cartoon here before you add it to the
list in the code.

Checked 2026-10-24 (the first four) and 2026-10-27 (the colour ones). This is a
record of what was checked, not legal advice.

## The rule they all rest on

In the United States a film published in 1930 or earlier is in the public domain:
the longest term a published work of that time could hold is 95 years, and it ran
out on 1 January of the 96th year (17 U.S.C. §304). Works from 1928 were freed on
1 January 2024, from 1929 on 1 January 2025, and from 1930 on 1 January 2026
(Duke Law's Center for the Study of the Public Domain, Public Domain Day 2024–2026:
https://cspd.law.duke.edu/publicdomainday/2026/).

Outside the United States: most countries in the Berne Convention, the EU and the UK
among them, apply the rule of the shorter term to a work from a country outside
them, protecting it no longer than its own country does (Berne Art. 7(8); EU Term
Directive Art. 7). Not every country does, so status abroad is likely but not
certain.

What we changed in each copy is cutting, scaling, smoothing the grain, greying and
removing the sound. That adds nothing anyone could own. Each soundtrack is removed
completely: the app has no use for it, and a soundtrack can carry its own
rights (a song or a recording) apart from the picture's.

## The rule for the ones after 1930

A film published from 1931 to 1963 is in the public domain in the United States if its
first 28-year term ran out and nobody filed to renew it (the Copyright Act of 1909;
17 U.S.C. §304). A renewal had to be filed in the 28th year, and the Copyright Office
printed every one in the *Catalog of Copyright Entries*. So for each later film the
check was to find its release, and then look for its renewal in the volumes for the
years it could have been filed. The volumes are on the Internet Archive as machine-read
text (Third Series, Parts 12-13, Motion Pictures and Filmstrips, 1961 to 1971, items
`catalogofcopyr3151213libr` and its neighbours; the 1934 to 1936 registrations are in
`catalogofcopyrig71libr`, `…81libr`, `…91libr`). Each title on the shelf was searched in those
volumes, and none has a renewal. A machine-read search can miss a line, so this is
the standard test (Peter Hirtle's chart, Cornell) done carefully, not proof; for the
two ComiColors, whose own catalogue entries also could not be found, the second
source is Wikipedia's note that most of that series was never renewed.

## Caveats

- **Trademarks are not copyrights.** Disney still holds trademarks in Mickey Mouse,
  and a trademark lasts as long as it is used. Showing the 1928 film is fine; what
  a trademark forbids is using Mickey (or Disney's name or logo) in a way that says
  Disney made, sponsored or endorsed ChromaGlass. So: no Mickey in the app's
  branding, store pages, icons or marketing, and the cartoons are listed by
  title, not by character. The Duke centre's note on this:
  https://cspd.law.duke.edu/mickey/. The same goes for "Silly Symphony", which is
  only named here as the film's history, never as a label in the app.
- **Only these versions are free.** Later versions of a character (Mickey as drawn
  after 1930, Fleischer's later Betty Boop and Bimbo) are still under copyright.
  Nothing new is drawn from these films; they are only played.
- **Restorations.** The Skeleton Dance's online copies all appear to come from a
  modern digital transfer, not from a print scan; no print scan of it is online.
  In US law a copy has its own copyright only if it adds original authorship, and
  a faithful transfer or clean-up of an old film adds none (the reasoning of
  *Bridgeman Art Library v. Corel*, 1999, for exact copies of public domain
  works), but this is the one copy here that is not a scan of an old print. If a print scan surfaces, swap it in.
  Steamboat Willie was taken from the 16mm print scan rather than one of the
  higher-resolution copies that came from a studio restoration.
- **Not used:** colourised copies (new authorship), "4K remaster" or "AI upscale"
  copies, and anything from Disney+.
- **The colour ones are copies of copies.** Each is a web transfer of an old print, so
  none has the clean colour of a restoration; that is the price of picking copies whose
  source is a print rather than a studio's remaster (the Archive also holds a Blu-ray transfer
  of Jack Frost and a "restored" Balloon Land with an introduction added, both passed
  over: a restoration's clean-up adds no authorship, but a print is what the Skeleton
  Dance note above prefers, and an added introduction is new material).
- **Considered and left off:** *Poor Cinderella* (1934, Betty Boop in colour; the film is
  not renewed but the character is a trademark that is enforced), the Popeye colour
  specials (the character's trademark, and some of the series' renewals are in the
  catalogue), the Superman shorts (1941 to 1943; the character is DC's trademark and the
  status is argued), *The Adventures of Prince Achmed* (1926, tinted) and Ruttmann's
  *Lichtspiel Opus 1* (1921, tinted), which are free by date but whose Archive copies
  are modern restorations or carry an uploader's licence claim (PLAN 14ac-3), and the
  1933 to 1934 ComiColors listed above, which were renewed.
- **Characters in the colour ones.** *Fiddlesticks* has a mouse that is a parody of
  Disney's, drawn by the man who drew the original; it is a 1930 film and free, and the
  Mickey rule above still holds: the shelf names the film, never the character.

## How the files were made

```sh
# cut the cartoon, crop the film's edges where needed, 24 fps, 384 px wide,
# smooth the grain, grey (the first four; the colour ones leave format=gray out),
# silent, index at the front for streaming
ffmpeg -ss START -to END -i SOURCE -an \
  -vf "[crop=…,]fps=24,scale=384:-2:flags=lanczos,hqdn3d=6:5:14:12,[format=gray,]format=yuv420p" \
  -c:v libx264 -preset slower -tune animation -crf 33 -profile:v high -level 3.1 \
  -g 96 -movflags +faststart OUT.mp4
```

The Skeleton Dance and Steamboat Willie at CRF 33, Swing You Sinners at 35 (its motion and grain cost the
most), The Tantalizing Fly at 32. Sizes: 6.1, 5.5, 8.1 and 1.5 MB (MiB), 21.2 MB for the
four. The first pass at 480 px with light smoothing came to 48 MB.
The colour ones, 2026-10-27, at CRF 33 (Gulliver's Travels, 74 minutes, at 36): Fiddlesticks 4.0 MB,
Jack Frost 7.3, Balloon Land 6.3, Somewhere in Dreamland 6.5 and Gulliver's Travels 27.6 MB
(MiB), 72.9 MB for the nine. Jack Frost is scaled to 4:3 (384×288): its 720×576 PAL frame
is not square-pixel. The colour is the print's own; the smoothing is the same as the grey ones'.
