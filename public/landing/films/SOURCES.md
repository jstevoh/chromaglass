# The music in the webpage's films and the channel's

Each card in the webpage's row of films is one of the app's looks playing a whole piece of
modern ambient music (`src/lib/siteFilms.ts`, `npm run sitefilms`, PLAN 34a-6 and 34a-6d). The
whole film is on the ChromaGlass channel and plays on the webpage from there; the TV app plays the
same films. The loops in this folder are cut from those films and have no sound. The channel also
has a long film of a whole album, a look for each piece. This file records where each piece comes
from and why we may use it. Add a piece here before you add it to the list in the code.

Checked 2026-11-21: each page was read that day and carried the licence line given below. This is
a record of what was checked, not legal advice.

Until 2026-11-21 the films played six 1920s 78 rpm records, public domain in the US. Those films
are still on the channel; the record of them is in `docs/plan-shipped.md` §34a-6.

## The licence: CC BY 4.0

Every piece is under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0),
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. It lets anyone copy, adapt and publish the music,
for any purpose, commercial use included, so long as the work is credited, a link to the licence
is given and changes are indicated. A film is an adaptation: the music is set to pictures, trimmed
at its head and tail and faded in and out. Nothing else is done to it (no EQ, no level change).

No NonCommercial (NC) and no NoDerivatives (ND) licence is used: the channel is monetised, the
webpage sells the app, and every film is a derivative.

The credit goes in each film's description on the channel, under the film's player on the
webpage, and on the TV while it plays. Each composer's own wording is used.

## Scott Buckley: the webpage's six films

Scott Buckley's Creative Commons music library, https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/. His
"Using this music" page, https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/using-this-music/, says the music
is "made available under the Creative Commons 'Attribution 4.0 International' … (CC BY 4.0)
License (unless stated otherwise)" and is "FREE, even for commercial use, as long as attribution
is given". Each track's own page below states CC-BY 4.0 and links the licence, and none states
otherwise. The files are the 320 kb/s MP3s each page offers for download.

His credit line, which every description carries word for word:

> [Track Title] by Scott Buckley – released under CC-BY 4.0. www.scottbuckley.com.au

**The channel's claim system.** He registers his own library with YouTube's Content ID (through
We Are Era Music, "claimed on behalf of Scott Buckley"). Since 17 April 2023 his system reads a
video's description and places no claim when it names him ("Scott Buckley", "@ScottBuckley" or
"musicbyscottb"). A claim that comes anyway is released with his form,
https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/copyright-claims-release/, and disputed in the channel's
claim system as licensed under CC BY 4.0. Adding the credit after a claim does not release it on
its own. His licence forbids registering his music, or a film of it, with a fingerprinting
service, so these films are never put into Content ID.

## Chris Zabriskie: Cylinders, the channel's long film

*Cylinders* (2014), nine pieces for piano and synthesiser, https://chriszabriskie.bandcamp.com/album/cylinders.
The album's page states its licence as Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 and links
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. His page on using his music,
https://chriszabriskie.com/use/, says "CC BY 4.0", "Commercial use OK", and, to "Can I use your
songs in a video I make money from?", "Yes". He asks for the credit in the work itself where it can
be: the film's chapters name each piece as it plays, and the description carries his wording:

> "Song Name" by Chris Zabriskie / From the album Cylinders / Licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0

**The channel's claim system.** He does not use it: "If you receive a copyright claim on my music,
it did not come from me. Please contact me right away." A claim on this film is someone else's
copy registered without a right to it; dispute it as licensed under CC BY 4.0, with this file.

The files are the album as he released it for free in 2014, kept by the Internet Archive from the
Free Music Archive (item `Cylinders-15736`, https://archive.org/details/Cylinders-15736), VBR MP3
at about 330 kb/s. Each piece is played whole, from its first sound; the last ends on fifteen
seconds of silence, which is cut.
