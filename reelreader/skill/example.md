# A worked example

The request: *"Here's a take from last night's set (frozen-take.mp4). I'm a liquid light
artist. Is it any good?"* It is the test set's frozen take, so the right answer is known
([testset/README.md](testset/README.md)). This page shows the routine and the account it
should produce, with the honesty notes where they bite.

## What was run and read

```sh
reelreader frozen-take.mp4 --out /tmp/frozen
reelreader frozen-take.mp4 --out /tmp/frozen --at 7   # after the sheets: one still in the stillness
```

The Read as line said liquid by itself (one take, its own motion, a dark ground); for a take
the person says is a liquid show, `--liquid` would have been passed either way. Read in order:
`timeline.png`, both sheets, then `summary.md` from the top. The timeline's motion strip is
flat from 0:04.7 to 0:10.3 and its streaks run straight across there: nothing moves. The
summary's Overall says the opposite ("holds one steady pace from start to end"), and its
Footage section explains why: it dropped 0:04.8 to 0:10.2 as a "title span", so the motion
reads never saw the stillness. One still at `--at 7` confirmed the frame is the same as at 5 s.

The lens is the VJ's (honesty rule: say which). The card gives "Strongest on Intensity (8.0)
and Restraint (7.9)", the same verdict it gives this take unfrozen, so it says nothing about
the fault and goes last.

## The account

**Short answer: the take has a five-second freeze in the middle, and that is the thing to fix
before anything about the look.** From 0:04.7 to 0:10.3 the picture stops dead (the timeline's
motion strip is flat, and the frame at 0:07 is the frame at 0:05), then moves on as if nothing
happened. On a projected show that is the fault an audience sees first. The tool's own
summary misses it: it filed the stillness as a title card and then called the take "one
steady pace", so do not trust that line for this clip.

**What is on screen.** A round dish on black, red and violet dye bodies pressed against each
other and parted by dark seams, a few bright pink beads drifting through, small dark rings
(bubbles). A small bright ring with a dot sits top right around 0:07 to 0:08 (sheet 1): a
pointer or tool mark over the picture, worth hiding for a show.

**How it moves, outside the freeze.** Flowing, at a steady pace: the fluid's own speed is
2.8% of the width a second (real shows read 2.0 to 8.0), the whole picture moving together is
only 22% of the motion, so it is the liquid moving, not the dish. There is no swell in 16 s,
but swells are one take's measure (may vary from take to take) and 16 s is too short to judge
an arc.

**The look.** Highly colourful (113 on the Hasler–Süsstrunk scale), two hues 90° apart, red
and violet, on a dark ground (43% near black), soft edges and cells. A strong look for a
small palette.

**Not judged.** No audio track, so nothing about the music. The Footage section also masked
four regions as "still overlays" that the sheets do not show; the design numbers leave those
regions out. The card's scores are calibrated on two real shows and give this broken take the
same verdict as the unbroken one, so they are left out of the verdict.

Sheets: `/tmp/frozen/sheet-1.png`, `/tmp/frozen/sheet-2.png`; timeline `/tmp/frozen/timeline.png`;
still `/tmp/frozen/still-7.00s.png`.

## Why it is written this way

- It leads with what was asked (is it good?) and the fault that decides it.
- Every claim has its number or its frame; the one the tool got wrong is called wrong, with
  the reason (rules 1, 2 and 4).
- The card is quoted with its calibration and its blind spot, not as a verdict (rule 5).
- One take's measures are hedged, and what could not be judged is listed (rules 3 and 7).
