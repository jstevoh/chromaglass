# 41. Specs for the thin items, part 2: sound and beat, many plates, the fluids

**Written 2026-11-01**, with the same reading rules as part 1 ([`40-specs-frame-gig-desk.md`](40-specs-frame-gig-desk.md), "How to read an entry"): *(read)* is a count from the code to be re-measured by the first run, *(measured)* is a printed number from an earlier PR, *(bar proposed)* is the bar the entry asks for, pinned by whoever writes the check, and **a check marked *owed* is written by Claude first, shown failing on main; the builder never writes or loosens a check.** The fluid entries are physics: they are Claude briefs first (the split), and each says the phenomenon it must simulate rather than imitate.

## Lane D: the beat and the song (Claude writes the check first for every entry)

The home of 10.2-* and 10.3-* is `docs/plan/10-playing-like-a-show.md`: **10.2** is step 2, "Hear the song's shape live", and its "found on the way, not yet fixed" list; **10.3** is step 3, "Choose the accents", and its "found while building it, not yet done" list. The checks are `npm run shape`, `breakdown`, `songfollow`, `kicks`, `downbeat` and `learn`. Every figure below is that list's own.

- **5-downbeat · Sound learn's "Each bar" fires on the bar grid's one** (D, S). `soundLearn.ts` fires `bar` on every fourth beat counted from the clock's lock (`beatIndex % 4`), so it lands on the one only a quarter of the time. When the bar grid is sure of its one (the grid's own 0.15 mark), fire on the grid's one; when it is not, keep the count. **Pass:** `npm run learn` with a song whose lock begins on beat 3: after the grid is sure, the bar fires on the one in at least 95 % of bars (bar proposed), 0 bars fire on any other beat; before it is sure, firings are identical to main's; sound learn's other lines unchanged. **Check:** `learn` exists; the lock-on-3 song is *owed*. **Phone:** none (same trigger list).
- **10.2-shelf · A recorded song with a beat, on the shelf** (D, S, a person supplies the file). Nothing on the shelf (`src/lib/musicLibrary.ts`, `public/music/`) has a beat: its four tracks are ambient, so the shape tracker is measured on synthesised songs and, on the shelf, only for not inventing drops or builds. A CC-licensed dance track with known sections, and a CC-licensed rock track with known bars (also for 10.3-sure). **The builder does not choose or fetch audio**: the owner or Claude supplies each file with its licence text and a sidecar of annotated sections and bar ones; the builder adds the track to `LIBRARY` with its credit (`credits()` prints it), wires the sidecar to the check, and touches nothing else. **Pass:** `npm run shelf` plays the new track and reads its bands (the existing line), and the shape check on it reports each annotated drop within one bar of the annotation (bar proposed: 1 bar) with no drop or build in an annotated steady section. **Check:** `shelf` exists; the annotated line is *owed*. **Phone:** the shelf is the same list on the phone.
- **10.2-snare · A snare roll is not a run of kicks** (D, S). The kick onset reads a fast snare roll's 200 Hz body as kicks: in the club song's build, 32nd-note snares fired `kick` three to five times in a half-second, so sound learn on the kick fires through every build. `24-modular-rigs.md` (24.3-place-3) finds the same fault from the other side: the snare source fires on a lead's attack, and says the snare test should check the harmonic-to-percussive balance before it believes one. Test that balance (the onset's energy in the tonal, median-filtered spectrum against the broadband burst) before a kick or a snare onset is believed. **Pass:** in the club song's build `kick` fires at most 1 time in 2 s (it fired three to five times in a half-second, so 12 to 20 in 2 s, *measured*), `kicks`' 95 % onset hold in every other section unchanged at 48 and 44.1 kHz, and 24.3-place's 8 of 15 scale notes called snare falling to at most 1 of 15. **Check:** `kicks`, `music` exist; the build's false-kick count and the scale's snare count are *owed*. **Phone:** none.
- **10.2-build · A build is heard earlier** (D, M). A build is recognised about halfway in (the club song's four-bar second build at 65 %): the hats leave before the riser has climbed, so the top end falls before it rises, and the climb has to show in every third of six seconds so that a fill's two-beat jump is not one. A build that starts by taking the beat away is heard as a breakdown for its first bars. Add onset density (a snare roll doubling in rate) as a second reason to believe a build, and let a build that starts from the beat being taken away be re-read as a build when the density rises. **Pass:** recognition by 40 % of the build's length on the club song's two builds (it is 50 % and 65 % now; bar proposed), 0 builds called in the steady sections of the check's songs, and a fill's two-beat jump still not a build (the check's fills). **Check:** `shape`, `breakdown` exist; the 40 % line is *owed*.
- **10.3-fast · Above 148 bpm, the grid hears half tempo** (D, M, with 14t's clock half tempo, one pass). On four on the floor above about 148 bpm the bar grid names 75 to 87 bpm and is almost never sure of the one: 0 of 172 estimates at 160, 31 of 184 at 150 *(measured)*. It fails safe (every kick is a 1) but no accent reaches drum and bass or fast techno. Teach the tempo prior that the double of a settled half is a candidate, and do the clock's half-tempo fix (14t, `beatClock.ts`) in the same PR since the two fail together. **Pass:** at 150, 160 and 174 bpm on the check's four-on-the-floor songs, the grid names the true tempo (within 3 %) on at least 90 % of settled ticks and is sure of the one in at least 70 % of estimates (bar proposed; the slower songs get 82 to 88 %); the check's songs extended past 140 (a check change, Claude's), and every existing line unchanged. **Check:** `downbeat`, `kicks`; the fast songs are *owed*.
- **10.3-4otf · Four on the floor's one comes sooner** (D, S, after 10.3-fast). With the kick on every beat and a chord a bar, only the chord tells the one from three; on sixteen-bar sections the grid is sure of it 49 % of the time at 90 bpm and 82 to 88 % faster, and a third of the time on eight-bar sections *(measured)*. A crash on the one and the phrase's changes are evidence the grid does not use; the bass line's pickup it hears only as the harmony moving (a loop with one reached a t of 2.6 on it alone). Add the crash-on-the-one and the phrase change as evidence. **Pass:** sure of the one in at least 70 % of estimates at 90 bpm on sixteen-bar sections and at least 50 % on eight-bar sections (bar proposed), the false-sure rate (sure of a beat that is not the one) not above main's, printed both. **Check:** `downbeat`; the two bars are *owed*.
- **10.3-sure · The sure mark on recorded songs** (D, S, after 10.2-shelf). The sure mark 2.2 rests on three synthesised loops; it stands a third over their worst (1.65). A real bar-less track (a DJ tool, a techno loop with a drifting pad) may reach further. Run the grid on the 10.2-shelf rock and dance tracks with their annotated ones. **Pass:** false-sure at most 5 % of estimates on the recorded tracks at the mark 2.2 (bar proposed); if the mark has to move, both sets (the three loops and the recorded tracks) print at the old and the new mark in the PR, and the mark only moves if the loops' sure-rate stays at least 95 % of main's. **Check:** *owed* (needs 10.2-shelf's file). Do not start without a supplied file.
- **10.3-half · Half tempo, now and then** (D, S). Club 140 names 70 bpm on 2 of its 181 settled ticks (the check holds 95 %); the weighting toward 120 is an octave and a bit wide, and 140 is near where its double and half weigh alike. Narrow the prior or weigh the double by the onset density at the half. **Pass:** club 140's half-tempo ticks 2 → 0 of 181, every other song in the check no worse, `kicks` unchanged. **Check:** `downbeat`; the 0 of 181 bar is *owed* (the existing 95 % is not loosened).
- **10.3-newsong · A new song after a gap finds its one sooner** (D, S). The new song's one arrives at bar 14 of its groove after a gap, against bar 7 when heard alone, because the old grid is held four seconds before it is let go and the new one then gathers from nothing. Let the old grid go on the song-gap detector's event, or start the new grid's gather while the old one is held. **Pass:** the new grid sure of the one within 1.25 times the alone figure (bar 9 against bar 7; bar proposed), the old grid's hold unchanged when the gap is a breath and not a song. **Check:** `downbeat`'s new-song case; the 1.25 bar is *owed*.
- **10.3-fill · A fill is seen through the clock's prediction** (D, S). The beat clock fires on through a fill on its own prediction, which hides the fill from the accent's "after a fill" in the app (the check hears the kicks themselves). Feed the accent the heard kicks, not the clock's predicted beats, for the fill test. **Pass:** with the clock running in the app path (predicted beats), the fill is detected in every fill of the check's songs, as it is from the kicks (the app-path variant of the check equals the kicks-only run, fill for fill). **Check:** `downbeat`; the app-path variant is *owed*.
- **10.3-rest · The rest of the accents** (D, M, last of the 10.3 list). Four accents the step asks for and `downbeat` does not build: *every other bar* (the grid knows the bar, not yet the pair of bars), *fills only* (the fill could be the only press), *a hand's variation* (the depth varied a little from press to press), and *a hand-played press pulled onto the predicted beat*. A setting `accentWhen` (Every bar, Every other bar, Fills only) beside the existing accent choice, default today's; `accentVary` (0 to 1) for the depth variation, default 0; `pressSnap` (0 or 1, in ms of capture window up to 120) default 0. **Pass:** Every other bar fires on 100 % of the selected bars and 0 % of the others over 16 bars; Fills only fires only after a detected fill, 0 elsewhere; at `accentVary` 1 the press depth varies by 15 % either side with the mean within 2 % of the set depth; a hand press within 80 ms of the predicted beat lands within 10 ms of it with `pressSnap` 1, and a press further than 80 ms from it is not moved. The setting reaches the desks, the phone, MIDI and the remote (the `setting-auditor` agent runs on the PR). **Check:** `downbeat`, `lift`, `squeeze` exist; the four lines are *owed*. **Phone:** the same three controls on the Sound sheet.
- **10-go · Go on the music, and a T-bar** (B, M). Text in `docs/plan/10-playing-like-a-show.md` ("Go on the music"): a Go immediate and its fade in seconds today (`sendLook` → `fadeSettingsTo`); the Mixer's takes already fade in bars (`barsToMs`). Add a Go that lands on the next bar, in 4, 8 or 16 bars, or at the next section the song's shape hears, with the fade in bars; and a T-bar, a learnable fader and one on the phone, that scrubs the fade from the look playing to the one armed with soft takeover, and a Go that completes it. On the phone a long press on Go arms "on the bar" with a ring counting down, and the Looks sheet gets the T-bar. **Pass:** a Go armed "on the bar" lands within one frame (16.7 ms at 60) of the grid's one on `downbeat`'s song, 20 of 20 Gos; "in 8 bars" lands on the eighth one; "at the next section" lands within one bar of `shape`'s section; the T-bar takes over from a hand at the picked-up value with no jump over 2 % of travel; a Go with the default (immediate) is byte-identical to today's. **Check:** `setlist`, `downbeat`, `desk` exist; the landing lines are *owed*. **Phone:** as above, in the same PR.
- **5-shutter · Exposure time in the camera** (B, M). Text in `docs/plan/05-playing-it.md` ("Shutter"): an exposure-time control that accumulates frames into the camera's scene buffer with a decay, downstream of aperture and bloom, off in the light-show style, on by default in the Photograph presets. **Pass:** shutter 0 is pixel-identical to today's (`npm run pixels`, `cameras`); with a bright dot moving 200 px/s, a shutter of 0.5 s leaves a trail whose length is 100 px ± 20 % (bar proposed; the lab measures the streak), and the trail decays to under 5 % within 1.5 exposures; the frame time with the shutter on at most 0.3 ms over main's at 512² (`stages`). It is a setting: the desk, phone, MIDI and remote all reach it (`setting-auditor`). **Check:** `cameras`, `pixels` exist; the trail length is *owed*. **Phone:** a slider on the Camera sheet.
- **14t-decks · The decks' own beat** (A, M, risk named). Text in `docs/plan/14-show-at-the-gig-2.md` ("The decks already know the bar"): Pioneer's Pro DJ Link and Denon's StageLinQ beat packets give the true bar and tempo, which the ear gets wrong. A `server/prolink.js` beside `artnet.js`, into the beat clock as an outside source with 14e's lead, "Decks" as a tempo source in the Sound sheet. The protocols are documented by their users, not their makers, so **the builder works only from a recorded packet capture that Claude supplies as a fixture** and never from hardware. **Pass:** the capture replayed gives the clock the tempo within 0.1 bpm and the bar's one within one beat packet of the capture's beat-in-bar of 1, on 100 % of the capture's bars; a garbled packet is dropped with 0 crashes (a fuzz line: 1,000 random packets); Decks off is byte-identical to today's. **Check:** *owed* (a `prolink` check on the fixture, Claude). **Phone:** the Sound sheet's tempo source lists Decks.

## The fluids (physics at the core: each a Claude brief, then a build)

Real physics rule: say in the PR which phenomenon the change simulates, measure it against that phenomenon, and write any shortcut taken as a new item.

- **RM-D · Spreading, and a first pour that is not a tenth** (C, M, `docs/roadmap.md` "D", `docs/bubbles-plan.md` D). Oil on clean water spreads to a monolayer; oil on oil sits where it lands. The state variable is surface coverage and the force is the gradient of γ, not γ; a pure gradient force added to the velocity is what the projection removes (the wall H6 hit three times), so the force must enter the divergence, the dye's transport or a multiply. Half of it exists for soap (`lib/liquidPhase.ts`: a per-liquid field with a Marangoni force on the soap channel, and a ceiling for a uniformly soaped plate); the missing part is what it is attached to: soap has a tension field and the dye does not, so a pour onto bare plate and a pour onto a covered one behave the same. And `repel` is a scalar per cell, not a pairwise matrix. **Pass:** (a) oil poured onto clean water spreads to at least 2 times its pour radius within 10 s, the same oil poured onto an oil-covered plate to under 1.1 times (bar proposed); (b) total coverage times area conserved within 1 %; (c) with a pairwise matrix, oil and water mix across their interface under 5 % after 10 s while oil and silicone mix over 50 % (bar proposed); (d) `npm run physics`, `liquids` and `soap` unchanged. **Check:** all four are *owed* (Claude's brief and checks first). **Phone:** none; a look that depends on it gets its phone control with it.
- **RM-F · Depth, and a plate that is wet everywhere** (C, M, `docs/roadmap.md` "F"). The roadmap's claim was that the gap field is a depth that only feeds the squeeze and that advection, diffusion and the projection are depth-blind; **Thin Gap (18a, shipped, on in every look since #248) has since put the Hele-Shaw mobility in the gap, so read `npm run thingap` and `docs/plan/18-physics.md` first and keep from this entry only what is left**: the clear carrier (a `dye.a` of zero means nothing is there rather than clear liquid, and drying, wet-plate optics and the oil saturation in RM-D all want that one field). **Pass:** a wet mask separates clear liquid from bare glass: a clear pool has wet 1 and dye 0, bare glass wet 0; drying lowers wet at a set rate (a pool dries to wet under 0.1 in the time the setting names, 10 % of that time either way); oil spreading (RM-D) reads wet; `thingap`, `lamp` and `milk` unchanged. **Check:** *owed*. After RM-D's brief, since both share the field.
- **10.5-bodies · More liquids as bodies** (C, M each pair, after 18d). Only oil is a body (Oil Bodies, #179): silicone lands in the oil field; soap, milk, ink and the other bottles are still properties that blend. Give each immiscible pair its own treatment (roadmap §I), one pair a PR, starting with the pair that has the largest visible difference (milk against oil, then silicone against oil). The oil with Oil Bodies at 0 still loses colour (MacCormack drops what the flux transport keeps): try the flux transport for any look with oil, and measure its cost. **Pass:** per pair, a settled drop keeps at least 85 % of its dye (oil on water with bodies kept 88 % *(measured)*), two colours stay two colours after a merge (hue 40° apart or more), the seam inside freshly merged bodies is gone within 2 s (a lab measure of the line's contrast), and `stages` shows each pair's cost, at most +1 ms a step at 512² (bar proposed). **Check:** `bodies` exists; the per-pair lines are *owed*.
- **H8 · More bottles** (C, M each, after the liquid field moves to the GPU). `docs/bubbles-plan.md` C: latex, oil paint, clear medium and glycol first; then fizz, salt, slime, cornstarch, bleach. Each is a liquid file (`docs/liquid-format.md`, `docs/liquids/`) with its density, viscosity, tension and miscibility from a named source. **Pass:** each new file loads (`npm run liquidfile`), orders against water as its numbers say (viscosity ratio within 25 % of the source's, `thick`), and its bottle appears on the desk and the phone (`panel`, `phone`). **Check:** `liquids`, `liquidfile`, `thick` exist. Blocked on the GPU liquid field; do not start before.
- **15h · The Comb** (B+C, M). Text in `docs/plan/15-tools-on-liquids.md` §15h: N parallel carries a stroke on the dye, the oil bodies and the ferrofluid alike, the tine count and spacing on Tool Amount; a second pass at right angles makes the chevron; on the phone each finger is a rake; OSC gets `/comb`. **Pass:** a lab check in the manner of `npm run ferrohands`: N parallel bands moved a stroke with the mass exact (within 1e-4 relative), and `npm run detail`'s hard-edge share after a two-way comb at least 5 % (the marbling reference reads 5.6 %, ours 2.4 %; bar proposed). **Check:** *owed*. **Phone:** each finger a rake.
- **9j · Spikes that follow the music** (C, S). A real ferrofluid speaker's spikes jump on the kick; the spikes answer the magnet's field now, so the maze's audio breathing can drive their height. Drive the field's amplitude from the kick through a sound cord (never whole-frame motion from sound: `frameSway` stays 0). **Pass:** the mean spike height follows the kick envelope with a correlation of at least 0.7 over a synthesised four-on-the-floor bar (bar proposed), at Audio Impact 0 the height is flat (range under 2 %), and `npm run spikes`, `maze` and `soundcords` unchanged. **Check:** *owed*. **Phone:** the cord is a patch on the Sound sheet already.
